How Rome Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

The best way to avoid a surprise leak is routine maintenance. We recommend that homeowners flush the tank once a year (more often with hard water), have the anode rod checked every three to five years, test the relief valve once a year to make sure it isn't stuck, and keep the thermostat around 120 degrees, which reduces the risk of scalding and slows mineral buildup. Tankless owners should plan on descaling every year in hard-water areas.

Equipment age matters just as much. With regular service, a standard tank typically lasts 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts acting up, like discolored hot water, popping noises, or a damp spot around the base, it's smart to start planning a replacement. Replacing on your own schedule is cheaper and causes far less stress than an emergency swap after a leak.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to excess pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both inexpensive fixes. The same drip ignored for months can keep stressing the tank until the tank itself fails, and a small repair becomes a full replacement and a cleanup. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and ruined floors.

Typical Water Heater Issues in Rome

Most water heater problems we see in Rome start with the water itself. Depending on the neighborhood and the water source, minerals can be part of the picture: dissolved minerals drop out as sediment on the tank floor, insulating the water from the burner, which produces the rumbling and popping many homeowners hear during a heating cycle. Tankless units aren't immune either: scale builds inside the heat exchanger, which is why regular descaling matters so much where the water is hard.

Then there's age. Once the sacrificial anode rod is gone, usually somewhere in the second half of a tank's life, the tank begins corroding from within, and a drip from the bottom seam often shows up next. The area has a mix of fuel types, natural gas and propane units in some homes and electric in others, which means we service both sides: pilot assemblies, gas valves, and flues on gas units, and elements, thermostats, and breakers on electric ones.

Every house is set up a little differently. A home on a private well can see more sediment and sometimes a sulfur smell in the hot water, which a different anode rod often cures. A tankless heater in hard water needs annual descaling. And an older install getting a new heater may need updates that weren't required when it went in: an expansion tank, a properly routed relief valve line, a drain pan, or strapping where required. All of these is fixable, and most cost far less to address early. If something seems off, a diagnostic visit from our team catches the small problem before it becomes an expensive one.
